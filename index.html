Why it's harder than it looks

          
Great on benchmarks, shaky on our warehouse

          
Models score well on tidy academic benchmarks like Spider, then drop sharply on messy, realistic ones like BIRD and Spider 2.0. Our warehouse looks like the messy kind. These are the usual troublemakers.

        

        

        

          💡
          
The big insight: most mistakes are context mistakes, not SQL-writing mistakes. Most of the work is feeding the model the right context, and that context (catalogs, models, query logs) is exactly what data engineers already look after.

Rule of thumb: the more a wrong number would hurt, the more the answer should go through governed assets (T3 or a certified T8). The more open-ended the question, the more retrieval (T4, T5) and self-repair (T6) matter. Very high volume or strict latency? That's when T7 fine-tuning earns its keep.
